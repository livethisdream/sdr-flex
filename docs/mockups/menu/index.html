One list, two kinds of row?

The gesture discriminator is settled — openMenu(x, y, selection) already exists in app.js, already filters by stream type and by whether a selection is present, and a third answer is a one-line change. This is the half that is not settled, and that I wrongly called free: view parameters are not operations. Every row in the catalog today builds a node; a colormap sets a value and rebuilds nothing. One list holding both means two row types with two handlers — and the open question is whether it still reads as one thing.

The rows are the real ones: 22 operations transcribed from web/src/engine.js with their stream types, and the view parameters from app.js:1018 and :1029. Ranking and the six-row fold are ADR-0039's.

Building it turned up something that shrinks the question. View parameters belong to the view, not to the stream — and app.js has exactly two this.view() === … blocks. Spectrum carries seven parameters and Time carries two. Bits, Bytes, Events, Flow, Audio, Export and Grid carry none. So the mixed list only ever exists on two views, and on one of them it is two rows; everywhere else the menu stays exactly what it is today. Switch the view control below and watch the row counts.

The merge is a Spectrum-view problem and nothing else. On Bits, Bytes, Events, Flow, Audio, Export and Grid the menu stays exactly what it is today, and the node menu never carries parameters at all. That is most of the risk gone — but Spectrum is also where the time goes, and real · Spectrum is a nine-against-nine split, which is the hardest version of “does this read as one thing.” Start the drill there.

The fold does its job throughout: six rows and 205 px at every combination, against the 500 px that ADR-0039 was written to fix.

The drill

Reading a menu is not the same as finding a row in it, and only the second one can be timed. This names a row — drawn from the whole list, folded ones included — and measures how long you take to click it. Switch treatments between runs; the numbers accumulate per treatment.

Two things about the ladder, both of which the first cut got wrong. Order is held constant across all four — whichever kind the gesture asked about leads, then ADR-0039's rank — so the rungs differ only in how much the seam between the two kinds is marked. And the fourth rung, not merged, is the null: the parameters come out of the list and sit behind one row that opens them in place. Without it the drill could only ever say which merge was best, never whether to merge at all. Opening that row costs a click, and the timer charges for it.

What a result would mean

No gap between the two columns — finding a parameter costs the same as finding an operation — means the list reads as one thing, and values and keys only is enough. A gap that a hairline closes means merge, but mark the seam. A gap that survives headings means the two kinds do not belong in one list, and not merged should win outright — if it does, the answer is that the menu carries operations and one row that opens the view's options, which is still one menu and still depth 1.

Two things this does not measure, and they matter: whether a row that builds a node and a row that changes a color being one click apart causes mistakes you would not catch here, and whether any of it survives a week of use. It is a fifteen-minute instrument, not a study.

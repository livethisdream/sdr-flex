One list, two kinds of row?

A menu holding operations and view parameters at once — one list, or two stuck together? Change the controls, run the drill, read the two columns. Everything explaining why is below.

The fold was the expensive part

“+ 15 more…” says nothing, so the only move is to open it and look. The fold defers the cost of a long list rather than removing it — you cannot tell whether what is down there is what you came for, so your attention stalls on a decision it has no information for. Two fixes, and they answer different halves of it.

Naming it. The fold row now reads “+ 14 operations · 1 view option”. The catalog already knows this; nothing new had to be invented, and the count and the kind fit in the row the bare count used to occupy alone.

And what is behind it at all. Naming the fold exposed something worse. Ordered by ADR-0039's rank, a bare click on an iq node puts seven view parameters on top and folds all fourteen operations; a selection drag does the mirror image, folding all seven view parameters. The fold was not holding a tail, it was holding an entire category — which is exactly why you could not guess what was in it. Switch fold to “both kinds” to see the alternative, where three of each sit above the line and the fold becomes a tail again.

Three ways to decide what folds

The fold control in the bar switches between them. It is not a setting the app would ship. A menu whose structure is configurable is a menu nobody can learn, and law 9's “the menu shows the key on the row” needs the row to be where you expect it; a preference for this belongs nowhere, least of all in the settings pane ADR-0019 already refuses to build. This is a decision made once, in the catalog. The toggle exists so the three can be seen side by side before it is made.

The trade is sharp. By rank, a selection drag shows all four demodulators, so if you know you want FM it is one click. By group, it shows Demodulate 4 ▸ and FM costs two — but nothing is hidden behind an unnamed edge, and the fold holds only more of what is already on screen. Rank wins when you know what you want; group wins when you do not. The hotkeys tilt it: f is FM whatever the menu is showing, so the case rank optimises for is the case the keyboard already covers.

Two things building it exposed, both the same failure one level down. The leading group expands inline — that is what keeps “Tune here” one click — and on a bare click the lead is View, whose seven members took all six slots and folded every operation. Three seats are now reserved. And the fold label, listing rows one at a time, produced “1 view option · 1 view option · 1 view option”; it aggregates by group now, biggest first.

Why this page exists

The gesture discriminator is settled — openMenu(x, y, selection) already exists in app.js, already filters by stream type and by whether a selection is present, and a third answer is a one-line change. This is the half that is not settled, and that I wrongly called free: view parameters are not operations. Every row in the catalog today builds a node; a colormap sets a value and rebuilds nothing. One list holding both means two row types with two handlers — and the open question is whether it still reads as one thing.

The rows are the real ones: 22 operations transcribed from web/src/engine.js with their stream types, and the view parameters from app.js:1018 and :1029. Ranking and the six-row fold are ADR-0039's.

Building it turned up something that shrinks the question. View parameters belong to the view, not to the stream — and app.js has exactly two this.view() === … blocks. Spectrum carries seven parameters and Time carries two. Bits, Bytes, Events, Flow, Audio, Export and Grid carry none. So the mixed list only ever exists on two views, and on one of them it is two rows; everywhere else the menu stays exactly what it is today. Switch the view control below and watch the row counts.

That is most of the risk gone — but Spectrum is also where the time goes, and real · Spectrum is a nine-against-nine split, which is the hardest version of “does this read as one thing.” Start the drill there.

The fold does its job throughout: six rows and 205 px at every combination, against the 500 px that ADR-0039 was written to fix.

Where the hotkeys land in this

At the key level the merge already happened. keys.js binds seven keys to eight operations — t a f s c l e — and its RESERVED list names what the rest of the keyboard already holds. Most of that list is view parameters: = - 0 are zoom, which is a view parameter by ADR-0019's own table. Operations and view parameters have shared one keyboard namespace from the start, with a test asserting the two sets stay disjoint. The menu is the surface that has not caught up.

Three consequences, and none of them is “so the merge is fine”.

what hotkeys doconsequence for the merge
Only 8 of 22 operations carry a key. The key badge cannot mark the seam. The right-hand column has three states, not two — a key, a value, or nothing — and a keyless operation is told apart from a parameter only by the absence of a value. Watch the keyed ops figure above as you change stream type.
A key names a list, and the type filter picks from it. s is SSB on iq and Stereo decode on real, which is not a collision because the two can never be offered at once. That rule does not extend to parameters. c is CW demod; colormap would want c too — and on an iq node both are valid simultaneously. The disambiguation that makes operation keys safe has no answer here.
An operation is a verb; a parameter is a value. One key, one applyOp — but a key cannot “do” a colormap, only cycle it or open its control. Zoom gets away with =/- because it is continuous and has a natural increment. Colormap and window have no such thing.

So the split that falls out is not the one the treatments test. Parameters with a natural increment already have direct manipulation — zoom on the wheel, dB range on the color bar — and want no menu row and no new key. Parameters that are a choice from a set — colormap, window, FFT size — cannot have a key at all, and the menu is the only place they can go. Which means the keyboard does not relieve the merge question; it decides which parameters the merge is even about.

How the drill is built

Reading a menu is not the same as finding a row in it, and only the second one can be timed. This names a row — drawn from the whole list, folded ones included — and measures how long you take to click it. Switch treatments between runs; the numbers accumulate per treatment.

Two things about the ladder, both of which the first cut got wrong. Order is held constant across all four — whichever kind the gesture asked about leads, then ADR-0039's rank — so the rungs differ only in how much the seam between the two kinds is marked. And the fourth rung, not merged, is the null: the parameters come out of the list and sit behind one row that opens them in place. Without it the drill could only ever say which merge was best, never whether to merge at all. Opening that row costs a click, and the timer charges for it.

What a result would mean

No gap between the two columns — finding a parameter costs the same as finding an operation — means the list reads as one thing, and values and keys only is enough. A gap that a hairline closes means merge, but mark the seam. A gap that survives headings means the two kinds do not belong in one list, and not merged should win outright — if it does, the answer is that the menu carries operations and one row that opens the view's options, which is still one menu and still depth 1.

Two things this does not measure, and they matter: whether a row that builds a node and a row that changes a color being one click apart causes mistakes you would not catch here, and whether any of it survives a week of use. It is a fifteen-minute instrument, not a study.
